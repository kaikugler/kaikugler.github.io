Computational Linguistics · Trier University

## Research Interests

My research focuses on how linguistic information is represented by machines. I am particularly interested in **mechanistic interpretability**: investigating the internal representations and computational mechanisms through which language models encode and process linguistic information.
A second focus is the use of **AI for linguistic research**. I explore how language models can help us investigate linguistic structure, lexical semantics, semantic change, and linguistic variation, and how their representations compare with human linguistic knowledge and theories of language.
Methodologically, I combine **quantitative linguistics, computational modelling, and representation analysis**. My work draws on statistical methods, contextual embeddings, dimensionality reduction, and the quantitative analysis of language-model representations.

## Background

In 2026, I completed my PhD in *Computational Linguistics* (Dr. phil.), and in 2016 I graduated in *Linguistische Datenverarbeitung* and *Germanistik* (M.A.). I have taught Computational Linguistics at Trier University since 2017, as a research associate and as a lecturer, and I have contributed to several research projects as a member of the *krAil Research Group*. Since 2018, I have also maintained the department's IT infrastructure, including its GPU servers.
